# Limitations and threats to validity

Every number in this repository was produced by the method described in
[VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md). This document states what that method does **not**
support, in one place, so a reader does not have to reconstruct it from individual use
cases. Where a limitation has already changed a published conclusion here, that is noted —
those are the ones worth taking seriously.

## 1. The worlds are synthetic (the big one)

Every scenario is generated by a seeded rule set. Nothing in this repository is production
traffic.

This is a deliberate trade, not an oversight. Synthetic worlds buy two things that matter
more here than realism: ground truth is **known by construction**, so scoring is exact
rather than a judge model's opinion, and every published number can be **reproduced by
anyone at zero cost** on free tiers. A corpus built on proprietary production data would
have neither property.

What it costs: **these results do not license claims about production behaviour.** An agent
that scores 0.822 here has scored 0.822 on this world under these rules. Treat the numbers
as measurements of agent behaviour under fully specified conditions, and treat the
*patterns* — not the absolute values — as the transferable part.

## 2. The rules are authored, and a different author would get different numbers

Gold answers come from a policy the use-case author wrote. A domain expert would write
different thresholds, weight clauses differently, and obtain different accuracies. The
rules are committed and legible precisely so this is auditable: disagreement about a score
becomes a disagreement about a specific rule you can read, rather than about a grader.

Where a use case encodes a real external rule (a statutory caption requirement, a
regulatory scope-of-practice limit) that is cited in the use case's README. Most rules are
plausible rather than authoritative.

## 3. Sample sizes are small and the intervals are wide

The standard is typically 30 or 32 scenarios × at least 3 repeats per model per use case.
Bootstrap 95%
intervals frequently span 0.15–0.25. Two models separated by a few points are **not**
distinguishable, and the tables should not be read as rankings when intervals overlap.

Confidence intervals are reported everywhere rather than omitted, which is the mitigation,
not a fix.

## 4. Multiple comparisons are not corrected

Across 47 use cases, several metrics each, and up to five models, the repository performs
many implicit comparisons. No family-wise or false-discovery correction is applied. Some
"findings" at the level of a single use case will be noise.

This is why the [failure taxonomy](FAILURE_TAXONOMY.md) is organised around patterns that
recur **independently across domains** rather than around any single result. A pattern seen
repeatedly in unrelated services is more robust to this concern than one surprising number.
Single-use-case results should be read as observations, not established effects.

## 5. Some results ran against floating model aliases

29 of the committed evaluations predate provenance capture and were run against identifiers
such as `mistral-small-latest` and `Qwen/Qwen3.7-Plus`, which resolve to different weights
over time. **Those numbers are point-in-time observations and cannot be exactly reproduced**,
including by the author.

Results produced after provenance capture record the model the provider actually served, and
label themselves when the provider returns only a floating alias. This limitation shrinks
over time but does not retroactively resolve.

## 6. Cost is list price, not spend

Costs are computed from measured token usage priced at published rates. On free tiers the
actual spend is zero while the reported cost is non-zero — reported cost answers "what would
this cost at list price", which is the decision-relevant figure, not "what did this cost me".
Prices are cached in the harness and drift as providers change them; for aggregator-served
models the rate is fetched from the provider's published API instead.

## 7. Providers are non-deterministic and not under our control

Sampling is non-deterministic, providers silently update served models, and rate limits and
outages perturb runs. Repeats are the mitigation. The harness refuses to save an evaluation
whose runs failed at the transport layer, because such a run measures the provider rather
than the model — a safeguard added after two providers failed mid-experiment and produced
complete, well-formed results of zero that were indistinguishable, in storage, from a model
failing every scenario.

## 8. The mock backend is not a model

Each use case ships a deterministic scripted backend so the pipeline can be exercised with
no API key at zero cost, and it is built with an engineered gap so failure-reporting paths
are exercised. Mock numbers appear in `results/` and in charts labelled as such. **They are
a pipeline check, not a model evaluation**, and should never be read as a capability claim.

## 9. There is no human baseline

No use case measures how often a human expert performs the same task correctly. Accuracies
therefore have no ceiling to be read against: 0.62 might be excellent or poor for a task,
and this repository cannot tell you which.

## 10. Single author, and the author chose the deceptions

Every use case, rule set, and adversarial case was designed by one person, who also
interpreted the results. The mitigations are that scenarios, gold rules, results and
transcripts are all committed for inspection; that predictions are stated before runs and
recorded when falsified; and that corrections stay in the repository rather than being
quietly edited away.

Three conclusions published here were later found to be wrong by subsequent checks and are
documented as failure modes rather than removed: a defence that appeared to beat its control
but was an artifact of a model stalling more in the control arm; a model that appeared robust
to corrupted data but had never read the corrupted field; and a scenario generator whose
per-process hash would have made its own scenarios irreproducible. This is offered as
evidence the method catches its own errors, not as evidence it catches all of them.

## 11. Primary-source grounding is not a compliance determination

The decision-gate labs cite current agency or eCFR anchors and record the retrieval date,
but their executable policies and records are deliberately fictional. A source can support a
narrow synthetic gold label without establishing scope, jurisdiction, exceptions, operator
procedures, or production fitness. The research also found a concrete example of this risk:
a suggested draft EU GMP annex was not present in the current final EudraLex annex index and
was removed as an operative premise before implementation.

No benchmark result authorizes batch release, grid energization, a hiring action, aircraft
dispatch, sanctions or SAR action, or tax filing. A production adaptation needs a qualified
domain owner, dated source capture, legal or safety review where appropriate, change control,
privacy and security assessment, and independent validation.
