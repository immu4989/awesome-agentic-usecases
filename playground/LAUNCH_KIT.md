## LinkedIn launch post

> AI agents do not fail only by producing obviously bad prose. They can choose a plausible action while claiming evidence they never had, transfer a valid rule into the wrong exception, or let an urgent right expire behind a completed packet.
>
> I built **Can You Trust This Agent?**: a zero-install, $0 interactive evidence playground inside Awesome Agentic Use Cases. You review five committed real-model traces across grid restoration, hiring, pharmaceutical manufacturing, health-insurance appeals, and security operations. For each one, choose Trust, Verify, or Block before the contract-backed ground truth is revealed.
>
> Your answers stay in your browser. At the finish, the site generates a local, downloadable review receipt and a challenge link. It is deliberately not a certification or universal model score. Every result links to the exact scenario, model artifact, runnable lab, and a route to dispute the ground truth with primary evidence.
>
> Try it, share where your judgment differed, or contribute a counterexample through the open Challenge: https://immu4989.github.io/awesome-agentic-usecases/?case=grid-restoration-ready#playground

## Domain-review outreach

Send these only to people for whom the case is genuinely relevant. Personalize the first line, do not mass-tag, and ask for critique rather than endorsement.

- **Grid operations:** “I built a short, inspectable grid-restoration review case around a conjunctive re-energization gate. It is not operational guidance; it is a public evaluation artifact with the exact scenario, model output, and reviewer contract linked. I would value a critique of the deciding facts and ground truth.”
- **Hiring:** “This bounded hiring case tests whether an agent distinguishes a declared notice from proof of delivery. The tool never makes a hiring decision; it exposes the human-review boundary and links every claim to an inspectable contract. I would value a domain review of the evidence requirements.”
- **Pharmaceutical quality:** “This research case isolates a transfer failure: a rule valid for one test family becomes unsafe when the sterility fact changes. The artifact is educational, not a batch-release system, and preserves a human authority boundary. I would value a technical review of the scenario and assertions.”
- **Health appeals:** “This case tests a subtle failure: the primary packet can be complete while the companion urgent-review right is about to expire. It does not give medical or legal advice. I would value a review of whether the scenario makes the time-sensitive boundary clear.”
- **Security:** “This case demonstrates an environment-originated injection where the user message is benign and the tool metadata is the attack surface. The exact model trace and failure assertion are public. I would value a red-team critique or a stronger counterexample.”

## Honest launch checklist

1. Open the live link in a signed-out browser and complete all five cases.
2. Download the local result card and verify the score and five failure-shape rows.
3. Publish the X launch post with the main social card.
4. Publish the LinkedIn post with the same direct link.
5. Run one scenario post per day; use its dedicated card.
6. Invite five relevant domain reviewers individually, without mass-tagging.
7. Route disagreements through **Dispute the ground truth with evidence** so corrections become public artifacts.
8. Track only GitHub’s public traffic, stars, forks, substantive issues, and accepted contributions. Do not manufacture testimonials or engagement.

## Seven-day learning goals

- At least five completed reviews shared voluntarily.
- At least one evidence-backed ground-truth challenge or scenario correction.
- At least one independent Challenge submission or adaptation.
- Identify where people abandon the five-case path through direct feedback—not covert tracking.
- Decide which next trace to add from demonstrated reviewer demand, not raw impressions alone.
