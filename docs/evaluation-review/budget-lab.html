Operating-budget lab · fixed synthetic evidence

A good headline.
A failing service route.

Every task completes. Overall cost and p95 latency meet their limits. One specialist route still fails both.

This lesson separates quality policy from operating requirements. It keeps the failing group visible, preserves both verdicts, and carries the evidence into a combined CI gate. No API key or live agent is needed to read it.

Constructed scores, USD costs, and timings—not measured users, models, agencies, or production services. The “repaired” twin is a controlled fixture, not evidence of a real-world improvement. These thresholds are not recommendations.

01 · Inspect the distribution

What can a p95 leave out?

The fixture has 20 scenarios × 4 repeats = 80 attempts. The nearest-rank p95 selects sorted attempt 76. Its latency is 1 s—even though 4 attempts take longer.

A percentile is a summary, not a promise about every case. A group-level p95 can also hide outliers within that group; use a separately reviewed maximum-latency limit if every attempt must fit a ceiling.

02 · Keep the group checks

Both verdicts. Every scope.

In this fixture, each overall/group scope must stay at or below $0.5 per jointly passing attempt and 2 seconds p95. Costs include failures; a “passing attempt” is defined by the declared scoring criteria, not a verified business outcome.

Group membership and thresholds are supplied assumptions. The specialist group contains one scenario with four repeats, not a representative population sample. No fairness, production SLO, cost forecast, or deployment approval is claimed. The generic tool treats missing measurements as unverified rather than zero or a passing check.

03 · Compare without compensating

A cheap aggregate cannot buy eligibility.

The same two candidates enter a budget-aware matrix. Eligible for its recorded operating comparison: repaired. The aggregate-trap candidate remains visible with its exclusion reason; it is not silently removed from the evidence. One eligible candidate is not a vendor recommendation or proof of superiority.

04 · Recompute, then adapt

A result you can inspect offline.

  1. Download a dossier or matrix above and extract every file into a new folder.
  2. Use the matching current repository harness; these commands are not in published PyPI 1.6.0.
  3. Run the corresponding verifier below. The browser page does not verify itself.
python -m pip install -e harness

# Dossier: require BOTH policy and operating checks
aau evaluation-pack verify /path/to/extracted-dossier --require-budget

# Matrix: verify the complete descriptive comparison
aau candidate-matrix verify /path/to/extracted-matrix

The aggregate-trap dossier exits 1: its evidence verifies but the combined constraints fail. The repaired dossier exits 0. The matrix exits 0 even though it contains an excluded candidate—matrix verification is descriptive, not a deployment gate. Exit 2 means invalid or incompatible evidence.

Each dossier retains policy, baseline, candidate, diagnostics, the operating budget, separate verdicts, the combined gate, HTML, and a manifest. Verification recomputes all 14 files, not just hashes. The matrix has its own complete 14-file source chain. Keep the matching harness revision. Hashes are not signatures or authenticated execution.

Put your own reviewed limits into CI

Review meaningful cases and obligations, collect complete scored observations, and independently approve your baseline, quality policy, diagnostics, and budget. Protect their exact-byte approval hashes and workflow inputs outside candidate-controlled changes. Missing required evidence should not count as a pass.

  • Adapt the combined dossier for JSON, recorded observations, or CSV
  • Use the independently pinned CI budget gate
  • Read and reproduce the complete synthetic example

Only fixed synthetic fixtures are published here. General-purpose review tools retain unsanitized source details; inspect your own evidence for sensitive information before sharing. Nothing is uploaded automatically by the harness.

Research behind the lesson

Google SRE on service-level objectives and aggregation motivates examining distributions rather than relying on a headline. FinOps unit economics motivates explicit units and measurement assumptions. Neither source validates these fictional thresholds or establishes production suitability.
